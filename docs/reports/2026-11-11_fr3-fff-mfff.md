# FFF and M-FFF on the FR3 inverse-kinematics benchmark (`fr3_6d`, `fr3_5d`)

Question of this task: **on FR3, does the torus (M-FFF) beat Euclidean FFF?** Both geometries were
trained with identical settings (beta 400, the same y-noise, ~3M parameters, 6 residual blocks, SiLU,
1M train / 20k val, 50 epochs, batch 1000, Adam 1e-3, weight decay 1e-5, cosine, grad clip 10, seed 0);
the only difference is `--manifold euclidean` vs `--manifold torus --latent wrapped`. Sampling is one
decoder pass (1 NFE). Validation (`split='val'`, 200 poses) was used to check the runs and for the
y-noise decision; the numbers reported are on `split='test'` (1000 poses × 1000 samples). The
benchmark spec is `docs/robot_ik.md`; FMPE results are from `docs/reports/2026-11-11_fr3-fmpe.md`.

**Machine.** Same laptop as the FMPE report: NVIDIA GeForce RTX 3060 Laptop GPU (6 GB), driver 621.14,
CUDA 12.6, PyTorch 2.14.1+cu126, Windows 11, Python 3.12. All times are laptop-GPU times
(`--device cuda`). Every training run and every timed evaluation ran alone on the GPU.

## Summary

- **Both FFF geometries fail on FR3 with these settings.** Neither reaches a single pose within
  1 mm / 1° on `fr3_6d` (success 0 and 7e-6), and on `fr3_5d` success is 3e-4 and 2e-4. FMPE is far
  ahead on every accuracy metric: Err_post is 5–6× (6-D) and 5–7× (5-D) lower for FMPE.
- **On `fr3_6d` the Euclidean model ignores the orientation.** Its median rotation error is 101°; the
  rotation part of the re-simulation error is 3.95, what random orientations give (≈ 4). The torus
  model does somewhat better on rotation (64°) but worse on position (106 vs 48 mm).
- **Torus vs Euclidean, the direct answer:**
  - `fr3_6d`: the torus has lower Err_post (0.117 vs 0.135, difference 0.018 = 37× the 5e-4
    oracle noise) and much higher coverage (0.20 vs 0.03), but neither model is usable for IK, and
    the torus has the larger position error.
  - `fr3_5d`: the torus is **worse** (Err_post 0.024 vs 0.017, difference 0.007 = 14× the oracle noise;
    median axis error 16° vs 5°; coverage 0.79 vs 0.85; success at 10 mm / 5° 0.07 vs 0.11).
  - **Joint 7, where the torus was expected to matter, is a clear torus win.** On `fr3_5d` the
    Euclidean model makes the free joint 7 bell-shaped (edge bins at 0.36× the flat level, 8.3% of
    samples outside the limits, up to 9.1 rad); the torus model is nearly flat (0.83–1.05×, 2.5%
    outside, per-pose KS 0.034 vs 0.071, ground truth 0.027). Joint-7 limit violations drop from 8.4%
    to 2.2–2.4% on both tasks.
  - So: the torus fixes the one joint whose range covers nearly the whole circle, but this does
    not translate into a better posterior overall; on `fr3_5d` it is worse overall.
- **y-noise: 1e-3 for all four runs.** It trained stably to 50 epochs; 1e-2 was no better on
  validation (details below).
- **Calibration:** all four runs are calibrated per joint and jointly under-confident (signed error
  +0.10 to +0.14), the same pattern as on the toy problem.
- **Not converged.** The exact NLL is still falling at epoch 50 in all four runs (0.08–0.14 per epoch
  over the last five epochs). Training was not extended.

## Code changes (commit `8ea87b1`)

- `models/fff.py`: `ANGLE_DIMS['fr3_6d'] = ANGLE_DIMS['fr3_5d'] = (0, …, 6)` (T⁷, no Euclidean part).
  New `set_angle_lower(lower)`: `unembed` then returns each angle in `[lower_j, lower_j + 2π)` instead
  of atan2's `(−π, π]`. `build_model` in `train_fff.py` sets it from `robot_ik.kinematics.joint_limits()`
  for FR3 torus models. Every FR3 range is shorter than 2π, so the representative is unique; a value
  above `upper_j` stays a violation. Nothing is clamped or resampled. The window is a non-persistent
  buffer, so existing state dicts load unchanged. Round trip on FR3 training data: max error 3e-7,
  no sample outside the limits.
- `experiments/train_fff.py`: `--y-noise` is required when the problem has no `GT_SIGMA` entry (clear
  error instead of a KeyError). `GT_SIGMA` is unchanged.
- `experiments/evaluate_fff.py`: `--split {test,val}` (val results get a `_val` suffix), the
  reconstruction error is computed on the scored split, and FR3 results get
  `joint_limit_violation_per_joint` from the scored samples.
- `experiments/plot_fmpe_posterior.py`: the FR3 drawing code is now `fr3_marginals_figure`, used by
  both the FMPE and the FFF scripts. `experiments/plot_fff_posterior.py` draws FR3 runs with it (to
  `figures/fff/posterior/` or `figures/mfff/posterior/`), and with `--joint7` the pooled joint-7
  histogram for `fr3_5d`.
- `--redraw-nonfinite` was **not** added: no FFF evaluation produced a non-finite sample
  (`nonfinite_sample_fraction` 0 in every calibration run as well). Redrawn samples: 0 in all runs.
- **Toy check:** `evaluate_fff.py kin_fff_s0 kin_mfff_wrapped_b400_s0 --device cpu` after the changes
  reproduces the committed Err_post, Err_resim and reconstruction error to ~1e-10 (the committed
  toy result files were left unchanged).

## The y-noise

| Run (`fr3_6d`, Euclidean) | y-noise | Epochs | Val recon at epochs 1 / 10 / 20 / 30 / 40 / 50 | Final val exact NLL | Val Err_post | Val rot / pos error median |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `fr3_6d_fff_b400_ynoise1e-3_stopped_s0` | 1e-3 | 6 (stopped) | 1.9e-3 … 6.4e-3 at epoch 6 | 4.75 (epoch 6) | – | – |
| `fr3_6d_fff_b400_ynoise1e-2_s0` | 1e-2 | 50 | 2.1e-3 / 2.6e-3 / 4.0e-3 / 3.6e-3 / 2.2e-3 / 1.86e-3 | −4.45 | 0.1278 | 95° / 36.5 mm |
| **`fr3_6d_fff_b400_s0`** (chosen) | **1e-3** | 50 | 1.9e-3 / 3.3e-3 / 3.4e-3 / 2.8e-3 / 2.4e-3 / 1.58e-3 | −4.65 | 0.1300 | 100° / 48.4 mm |

What happened:

1. The first run with 1e-3 was stopped after 6 epochs because the reconstruction error rose every
   epoch (val 1.9e-3 → 6.4e-3, train 0.8e-3 → 3.2e-3), the stop rule of the task.
2. The retry with 1e-2 showed **the same rise** in the same epochs, then a plateau around 3e-3, and
   the reconstruction error fell only in the second half, as the learning rate decayed. So the early
   rise is how FFF trains on FR3 at this learning rate, not an effect of the small noise, and the
   1e-3 run had been stopped too early.
3. 1e-3 was therefore rerun for all 50 epochs. It followed the same course and ended with a lower
   reconstruction error and a lower NLL than 1e-2. The NLL never ran off to large negative values.
4. On validation the two are close (Err_post 0.130 vs 0.128, a 0.002 difference); both fail in the
   same way (orientation ignored). 1e-3 is stable, so it is the chosen value (the rule: the smallest
   value training allows). It was used for both geometries on both tasks.
5. `fr3_5d`: 1e-3 trained stably on the first try (val recon 2.3e-3 at epoch 1, 5.3e-3 at epoch 10,
   1.0e-3 at epoch 50), so no retry was needed.

- **Reconstruction error:** in all four runs it rises over the first ~5–20 epochs to 3–6e-3, then
  falls over the second half to 1.0–1.95e-3 at epoch 50 (toy runs ended at 2–4e-5). It was still
  falling at epoch 50. On the ground-truth test samples it is 1.42e-3 / 1.74e-3 (6-D Euclidean /
  torus) and 7.0e-4 / 8.6e-4 (5-D).
- **Convergence:** the exact NLL is still falling clearly at epoch 50 in every run (table). The FFF
  training objective is not monotone (it rises in the first half and its minimum is at epoch 1), so
  its log-log slope is a poor convergence measure; for completeness, the val-loss log-log slope over
  epochs 30–50 is −0.28 / −0.17 (6-D Euclidean / torus) and −0.37 / −0.28 (5-D). Training was not
  extended (team decision). The exact NLL is not comparable between geometries (different latent
  spaces), only along one run.
- The torus runs are ~23% slower to train (14 ambient dims instead of 7).

## Something that looks off: the wrapped latent is centred at 0

The wrapped-normal latent sits at angle 0 on every circle with σ = the training std of that joint. On
the toy problem all angles were centred near 0, so at initialization the torus model matched the
Euclidean model (`docs/fff.md`). On FR3, joint 4 has mean −1.6 rad and joint 6 mean 2.53 rad, while the
Euclidean model standardizes them to mean 0. So on FR3 the two geometries do not start from the same
distribution, and the comparison is not purely "geometry only" for those two joints. A rotation of
each circle by the joint's circular mean would restore that; it was not done here because it changes
the model being measured. Worth deciding before more torus runs.

## Open questions

- **Why FFF fails on FR3.** Both geometries learn the position roughly and the orientation poorly
  (6-D) or the axis roughly (5-D); the posterior is too wide jointly. Candidates, none tested: too few
  epochs (the NLL is still falling fast), beta 400 too weak for a 1-D posterior in 7-D, the y-noise
  (1e-3 and 1e-2 behave the same, so the noise level is not the main cause at this budget).
- **y-noise** (for Paul): the FR3 ground truth is noiseless; the lowest allowed value, 1e-3 (1 mm and
  ~0.06° on the rotation entries), trained stably and was used. Is training on noisy y acceptable
  for a noiseless benchmark, or should FFF be scored against a ground truth with the same noise?
- **Joint limits vs torus:** ~30% of FFF samples violate a limit in both geometries; the torus moves
  the violations from the wide joints to the narrower ones. No clamping, rejection or limit-aware
  latent was added.
- **Epoch budget:** all four runs were still improving at epoch 50. 50 epochs for comparability, or
  longer?
- **Latent centring** (previous section).
